# Provenance and honesty rules

The rubric spends as many words on what it can't claim as on what it can.
Source: the rubric's front matter, §A, §B.3 and §H.

## The archive chain only grows

- v1 "as induced" (2026-08-09) and v2 "as adopted" (2026-08-11) are each
  kept as a **byte-identical frozen fork**, identified by SHA-256 prefix
  (`3e63d226…`, `43dad6fc…`).
- v2 was forked **before** the v2.1 edits were made.
- "Nothing in it is deleted or rewritten" (operator instruction). Every
  amendment's numbers, and every discard with its numbers, live in an
  adoption record.

## Third-party protection

`captured` rows are real posts by real named people. The rubric cites them
by **record ID and topic handle only, never an author name paired with a
grade**, and that rule follows the rubric into every artifact built from it.
That is why the rubric is full of IDs like `recAq9lbjnK9h6EqZ`.

## Forbidden inferences

- **No intra-rater consistency number of any strength.** The duplicate-pair
  instrument was voided by the rater's own disclosure: the pairs carried
  identical titles and were graded in one sitting, so they measured
  recognition, not consistency.
- **No agreement statistic about the Great boundary.** It rests on one
  as-written data point; it is "a stated preference structure, not a
  measured one."
- **No comparison between signal types** (quick grades vs critiques vs
  rewrites). That question was de-scoped and is "not weakly answered here."

## Selection disclosures

- The graded order became assistant-curated at 14 of 39, with unblinded
  knowledge: not a random draw.
- The holdout excludes the 5 rewrite-source rows by operator ruling, so it
  under-represents posts that inspired rewrites.
- Validators were blind but **not operator-calibrated**; where their
  leniency differs from his (the slop boundary, the holistic gate), the
  rubric names the rows.

## Deviations are written on the criterion

When a criterion was adopted against a pre-registered rule, the deviation is
stated on the criterion itself, not only in a separate record, together with
its reversal path.
