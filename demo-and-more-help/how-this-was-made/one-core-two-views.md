# One core, two views

The rubric is "one file, one criterion core, two registers". §B is the
grading view, §C the writing view; everything else is "the record that makes
the two views honest." Neither view may introduce a criterion the core lacks.

## The grading view (§B) is the grader prompt

§B is written to be pasted, complete and uncompressed, into every grader
call. It is self-contained: column ontology, evaluation order, all 17
criteria with statements, validation numbers, sources and exemplar pairs,
and the tier boundaries.

**Evaluation order (B.1), fixed:**

1. Eight disqualifiers first, in ID order. Any hit ends at **Unpostable**,
   but the grader cites every hit found before stopping.
2. All eight clean → at least **Good**. Then the Great set.
3. **Reasoning before verdict.** Each criterion cited separately, as
   `[ID] "verbatim criterion statement"`: never blended, never from memory,
   never by line number.
4. Any verdict below Great cites at least one criterion ID.

**Exemplars are contrastive pairs**: a failing and a passing member, usually
the operator's own before-and-after, with one line stating the only
difference. They exist to pin the boundaries where blind judges wobble.

## Arithmetic, not judgment

Two criteria are mechanical. **WRAP-20** (no hook line over 85 characters) is
"run as arithmetic, not judgment", and the writing skill's pre-flight
enforces it before a grader is called. Since v2.1 the order matters:
**classify the hook first** (one opening block of 100 to 240 characters is
style 2; two blocks split by one empty line is style 1), then count.
Classifying afterwards reproduces the false positives.

## The voice-authority rule

Seven criteria are tagged `voice`: they encode the owner's style, not
quality. They may only downgrade content whose voice authority is the
rubric's owner. On third-party or client content an unmet voice criterion is
annotated `off-voice` and does not lower the tier; Great then rests on the
disqualifiers plus the holistic gate, the click-invitation criterion and the
length criterion. "Off-voice is different, not worse."

## The writing view (§C)

The same criteria as 14 ordered, positively framed instructions, from
"render-clean always" to "then read it as a stranger scrolling". Each step
carries the criterion IDs it serves, so a writer and a grader can't drift
apart.
