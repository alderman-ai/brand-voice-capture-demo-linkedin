# Taste can be written down

"Taste" sounds like the one thing you can't hand over. Most of it can be, in
three layers.

## 1. The mechanical floor

Rules a computer can check with arithmetic. From Alex's starting notes
(`starting-domain-expertise-from-operator.md`):

- **A hook line must not wrap.** At most 85 characters per line, emojis
  included: his own cutoff, "a safe cutoff that has some buffer for different
  rendering environments."
- **"Bold" means unicode bold** (𝗹𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀), never `**asterisks**`,
  because LinkedIn doesn't render markdown.
- **No em dash glued between two words** (word—word). Alex writes ` -- `
  instead, as a deliberate "Still Human" signal.
- **No flag emojis** (they show up as plain letters on Windows) and **no
  profession emojis** (they can split into two).

Break any of these and the post is Unpostable, however good the words are.

## 2. The voice

Things only judgement can see, so each one is pinned with a failing and a
passing example, usually Alex's own before-and-after of the same post. Four
of them:

- **The copy performs its meaning.** Slow pacing for a slow idea, instead of
  saying the idea is slow.
- **Emphasis is a budget.** Emoji, bold, caps, whitespace: each used a
  little; switch tools when one runs out.
- **Every bold has a role**, and the bold parts read on their own as a
  summary for people who skim.
- **🤡 targets peers, never buyers.**

## 3. One judgement call

The last gate: imagine the post in a busy professional's feed. Would they
stop scrolling, and would they read to the end?

The rubric keeps this because one post cleared every listed criterion and
Alex still graded it Good, not Great: "this would be a Good not Great post as
re-written. But it's illustrative." A checklist can't close the last gap, so
the rubric names the gap instead of pretending it isn't there.

## The result

17 criteria and three grades: **Unpostable** (he wouldn't publish it),
**Good** (publishable), **Great** (publishable and exceptional). Since this
version Alex has added a fourth, **Postable**, as the second of four. Most
clients are perfectly fine with Postable, and it takes very little to reach.
Good as a floor is mostly for marketing agencies.
