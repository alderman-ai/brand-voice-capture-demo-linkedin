# What this means for an agency

Three things in this repo matter to an agency more than to anyone else.

## 1. You don't start from zero per client

Alex's view from building this: most corporate brands are about 90% the
same, so per brand it is mostly a matter of tuning word choice. In
non-native-English markets in tech, the biggest dial is how many modern tech
words stay in English even in the local language. For the CEE region that is
the main thing to tune, "and that's very doable."

So the mechanical floor (character counts, rendering rules, emoji hygiene)
carries over between clients. Each client's rubric is the remaining part.

## 2. Your voice and a client's voice are separate layers

The rubric already has a rule for this. Its voice criteria may only lower
the grade of content whose voice belongs to the rubric's owner. When it
grades someone else's content, an unmet voice criterion is noted as
*off-voice* and does not lower the grade. In the rubric's words: "Off-voice
is different, not worse."

An agency with ten clients needs exactly that separation: one shared floor,
one voice layer per brand, and a grader that doesn't punish client A for not
sounding like client B.

## 3. You choose the bar

Most clients are perfectly fine with **Postable**, and it takes very little:
basically the expert's starting notes plus some minor refinements. **Good**
as a floor is mostly for marketing agencies, the ones who would look at a
corporate post the client calls "ok" and know it isn't.

## Where the hard work sits

Writing down the floor is quick. The rich signal, full rewrites with notes,
is the bottleneck, and clients won't produce it themselves. Getting the taste
out of the person who has it, in a form an agent can use, is the actual job.
