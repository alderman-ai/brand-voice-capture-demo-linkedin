# One post, before and after

The quickest way to see the idea is one real example from this repo: post
P-026. An AI wrote it, Alex graded it, critiqued it, and then rewrote it.
Everything quoted here is from `refinement-vector-1-critiques.md` and
`refinement-vector-2-full-rewrites.md`, where the full post and all of his
notes are.

## The AI draft: Alex graded it Good

Its opening lines:

> The QA step everyone skips 𝗶𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗯𝗲𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗽𝘂𝗯𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵. It's two weeks after.
>
> The ten-minute audit that found four problems in one client's pipeline ⤵️

## His critique

- "Hook lines look very close to wrapping. Can't confirm in this UI."
- "Hook is 100% ambiguous to what intended audience you want to click on
  this post. Lots of fields have QA."

## His rewrite: he graded it "lower Great"

> 🔎 Agentic workflows 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗤𝗔 𝗹𝗼𝗼𝗽𝘀 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗻𝗼 𝗹𝗼𝗻𝗴𝗲𝗿 "𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲". In 2026 they're non-negotiable. But not all QA is created equal. Here's how to go from intermediate to advanced 👇

Two of his rewrite notes:

- "sometimes you need to change word order so that you can have one unicode
  bolded string make sense as part of a bolded gist flow."
- "went from ⤵️ to 👇 at end of hook so the 🔎+⤵️ wouldn't match colors."

And the weakness he names in his own version: "weak closer."

## What it shows

The three layers from [Taste can be written down](taste-can-be-written-down.md),
on one post:

- **The floor is arithmetic.** His first note is about whether a line wraps,
  which is a character count.
- **The voice is a set of choices you can name.** Bold placed so the bold
  parts read as their own summary; an emoji picked for its colour next to its
  neighbour. Notes like these are where most of the rubric's version 2 rules
  came from.
- **The last call is a judgement.** Even his own rewrite is "lower Great",
  and he says why.

This is Alex's taste. With your own rubric, the notes would be yours.
