# Fine is the problem

AI doesn't write badly any more. It writes *fine*. That's the problem.

A model learns to write from what it read, and a lot of what it read was
written to rank, not to be good. Alex's grading notes put it bluntly: brand
taste is something you either extract from a domain expert or pay for,
"because model training data is horrible at this."

## What "fine" looks like

The rubric in this repo has a name for it: **generic AI-essay register**
("ai slop" in Alex's grading notes). Its tells, as the rubric lists them:

- an inversion opener: "Most X don't have a Y problem, they have a Z problem"
- a "Here's the pattern" pivot
- three tidy, parallel points
- a neat aphorism to close, sometimes with a bolted-on engagement question
- all in uniformly competent, sterile sentences with no individual voice

On the posts the first version of the rubric was built from, this was **the
single biggest separator**: 17 of the 22 posts Alex graded Unpostable failed
it.

## Plain is not the problem

One post in that set had zero emojis and zero bold, and still passed. It had
"Weird, right?", a lived client observation and an uneven, human rhythm. The
rubric's conclusion: slop is a missing *voice*, never a missing format.

## Why this hits agencies hardest

Fine is now cheap. What an agency is paid for is the gap between fine and
*right for this brand*. One of Alex's grading notes, on a corporate-sounding
post, puts the line exactly where agencies live:

> "Companies would probably say this post is ok. Agencies wouldn't."

If that gap only exists in a senior person's head, an agent can't use it,
and neither can a junior. The next page is about getting it out of the head.
