# why-this-is-useful/

**Why turning taste into a rubric matters to an agency: the idea behind this
repo, one short page at a time.** This is where line 1 of the assistant's
menu lands.

## The idea in ten sentences

AI already writes, and what it writes is *fine*: fluent, competent and
interchangeable, because it learned to write from the whole internet, SEO
filler and clickbait included. An agency doesn't sell fine. It sells taste,
and taste usually lives in one or two senior heads. Taste feels subjective,
but most of it can be written down: part of it is mechanical (a hook line
that wraps on mobile, bold that LinkedIn won't render), and the rest can be
pinned with named criteria and before-and-after examples. Written down as a
rubric, it does two jobs at once: a guide an agent writes against, and a
checklist an agent grades against. Once an agent can grade, a workflow can
loop (write, grade, rewrite) until a draft only needs last-mile edits from a
person. The rubric sharpens the same way: every grade, critique and rewrite
from the expert is new evidence, and any criterion that can't tell a good
post from a bad one gets thrown out. This repo is one real example, from
early in the process: Alex's taste for his own LinkedIn posts, turned into
17 criteria and three grades (Unpostable, Good, Great). It is a snapshot of
working files, and it is honest about its limits. The same method applies to
an agency's house voice, or to each client's.
